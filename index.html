The why.

My uncle spent his career in the United States Coast Guard — a chief warrant officer whose job was heading out when everyone else needed to get home safe.

This year, he was diagnosed with bladder cancer. He's the reason this site is named what it's named. Ours is a family that fights back, and we don't fight politely.

I can't take a single treatment for him. What I can do is run — farther than I've ever run in my life — and put every one of those steps to work. So from now until January, every training mile, every tailgate lot, every step on this page is pointed at one thing: raising as much money as possible for the fight against bladder cancer.

Where the money goes: we're finalizing the exact recipient as a family — the leading option is bladder cancer research right here in the Triangle. The moment it's settled, this page will link straight to the official fundraiser and say exactly where every dollar lands.

The game day tour.

Long runs don't take Saturdays off, and neither does college football. So the training route goes where the people are: through the tailgate lots on game day. Come say hi — I'll be the one still running at mile 20.

The training.

Getting a body ready to cover 100 miles in a day is its own long game — months of stacked weekends, back-to-back long runs, and a lot of very early alarms. The full week-by-week plan is coming to this page soon; the mile and step counters above are the live scoreboard in the meantime.
